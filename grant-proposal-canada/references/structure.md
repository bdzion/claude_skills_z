## Common sections and what reviewers look for
- **Summary / lay summary**: the problem, what will be done, for whom, why it matters, in
  plain language; no undefined acronyms.
- **Background and gap**: concise state of knowledge with recent citations; the specific
  gap this project fills (not a general literature review).
- **Objectives and methods**: each objective with its method, data, analysis and
  expected result; feasibility (pilot data, team skills, access to sites and data).
- **Work plan and milestones**: Gantt-style table by quarter; deliverables; who does what.
- **Team**: roles mapped to tasks; complementary expertise; prior collaboration.
- **Partner contribution and benefit** (partnered programs): contribution type and value,
  partner role in the work, benefit to the partner, IP and data arrangements.
- **Training (HQP)**: number and level of trainees, skills gained, mentoring plan,
  career pathways.
- **EDI**: concrete practices in recruitment, training environment and research design,
  following the funder's current guidance.
- **Knowledge mobilization**: audiences, products (briefs, tools, workshops, open data
  and code), timing, and how uptake will be tracked.
- **Budget and justification**: each line tied to an activity; eligible costs per current
  rules; partner cash and in-kind separated.
- **Risks and mitigation**: likelihood, impact, mitigation, trigger.

## Review checklist (before submission)
- [ ] Every evaluation criterion answered under a recognisable heading.
- [ ] Limits met with margin (`compliance_check.py`); fonts, margins and file formats per instructions.
- [ ] Objectives, activities, milestones and budget lines agree with one another.
- [ ] All numbers sourced or marked `[TO CONFIRM]`; all citations checked (DOI resolves to the cited work).
- [ ] Partner statements match partner letters.
- [ ] Acronyms defined at first use; lay summary readable by a non-specialist.
- [ ] Canadian spelling throughout (unless the template differs).
