# Communicating stress tests

## One-page concept note
1. The problem in two sentences: what kind of shock, why current information arrives too late.
2. What the stress test does: the question it answers for a named decision-maker.
3. How it works: shock register, propagation model, early signals, early-vs-delayed comparison.
4. What a decision-maker sees: one sentence per dashboard view.
5. Roadmap: phases with deliverables and partners (a roadmap persuades better than a
   budget line at the concept stage).
6. Why this team and why now.
Keep illustrative numbers out of a concept note unless labelled, in which case one
worked example is enough.

## Briefing note (government)
Issue, background, current status, considerations (options with trade-offs), next steps.
Neutral tone, no advocacy adjectives, no unexplained acronyms, sources in an annex.

## Dashboard or demo specification
For each view: the question it answers, the indicator(s), unit, data source and update
frequency, thresholds and what they trigger, and the uncertainty display. Typical views:
shock register (categories, ranked within category), buffer status (days of supply by
node), scenario explorer (choose shock and response day; see shortfall and avoided loss),
and signal monitor. Mark simulated or placeholder data on screen, not only in a footnote.

## Wording
- "Could avoid about X (range Y–Z) under these assumptions", not "will save X".
- Show the cost of acting early when the shock does not happen; it is the main objection
  decision-makers raise.
