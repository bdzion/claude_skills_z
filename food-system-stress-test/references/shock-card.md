# Shock card template

Fill one card per shock. Leave a field blank rather than guessing; blanks become the
research agenda.

```
Shock name:
Category: biological | input supply | infrastructure/cyber | trade/policy | climate/weather | financial | labour
Trigger and plausibility: (historical analogue with citation, or why it is plausible now)
Onset: sudden (days) | creeping (weeks to months)        Duration range:
First node hit:
Propagation path: node -> node -> node (with the dependency at each link)
Buffers on the path: stock (days of demand), spare capacity, substitutes, imports
Early signals: indicator, data source, update frequency, lead time before impact
Decision points: who can act, with what instrument, and how long it takes to take effect
Response options: (containment, capacity boost, rerouting/imports, demand management, income support)
Costs of acting early if the shock does not materialize:
Equity: who is affected first and most (low-income households, remote and northern
        communities, Indigenous communities, food banks)
Parameter sources: (one line per parameter: value, unit, source or "assumption")
```

## Example categories with Canadian relevance (to be sourced case by case)
- Animal disease in poultry or livestock affecting supply and trade access.
- Disruption of imported fertilizer inputs (phosphate, potash logistics, nitrogen and
  natural-gas prices).
- Cyber or power failure in refrigerated warehousing and transport.
- Border, rail or port disruption (labour action, weather, policy).
- Climate extremes affecting crops and transport (drought, heat, flooding, wildfire smoke).
